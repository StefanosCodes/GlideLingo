# GlideLingo emotional journey

## Current product truth

GlideLingo is a calm, focused Modern Greek learning client. The current repository has a local course catalog, enrollment, module path, course progress, a placeholder review queue, and a hard-coded seven-day streak. It does not yet have completed lesson playback, attempt evidence, mastery, scheduled review, analytics, a backend, reward inventory, social features, or production notifications.

The visual system is compact and neutral. Color communicates state; content creates hierarchy; motion is short and low-amplitude. Progress copy explicitly favors demonstrated real-world ability over XP. Proposed behavior design must not imply that unfinished systems already exist.

## Emotional north star: calm momentum

The learner should leave each meaningful interaction feeling one step more capable and knowing the next useful action. Emotional energy may rise for a real achievement, but the baseline remains calm, credible, and respectful.

| Journey moment | Likely starting feeling | Intended transition | Useful product signal | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Course discovery | Interest mixed with uncertainty | “This is for me” | Concrete real-world can-do outcomes and honest scope | Vague transformation promises |
| Enrollment | Commitment friction | Chosen direction | Preview, duration, first milestone, reversible choice | Forced commitment or invented scarcity |
| Before a lesson | Inertia or limited time | Readiness | One clear action, realistic minutes, visible payoff | Multiple competing rewards and actions |
| First challenge | Vulnerability | Safe effort | Permission to try, clear controls, low-cost first response | Early judgment or public comparison |
| Correct response | Relief and competence | Productive momentum | Specific confirmation, then quick continuation | Identical oversized celebration on every item |
| Incorrect response | Surprise or frustration | Clarity and agency | Explain the contrast, preserve dignity, offer a next attempt | Red failure theater, blame, or lost progress |
| Lesson completion | Relief and pride | Earned progress | What changed, evidence collected, what comes next | XP detached from ability or a dead-end trophy screen |
| Delayed review | Forgetting or uncertainty | Useful challenge | Why this item returned and what retrieval strengthens | Treating review as punishment or failure |
| Return after a pause | Distance, guilt, or confusion | Continuity | “Welcome back,” preserved progress, a small re-entry action | “You abandoned us,” broken identity, or threat copy |
| Meaningful milestone | Accumulated effort | Pride and learner identity | A specific capability, artifact, or before/after proof | Generic badge inflation |
| Course completion | Accomplishment plus uncertainty | Expanded possibility | Demonstrated abilities and a credible next path | Confetti with no evidence or automatic upsell pressure |

## Emotional intensity budget

Use intensity to preserve meaning:

- **Micro feedback:** under one second; informative; does not stop flow.
- **Lesson closure:** a short pause; summarizes earned progress and offers the next action.
- **Milestone:** distinct composition or motion; names the capability and may invite reflection or sharing.
- **Rare achievement:** memorable, accessible, and dismissible; never blocks access to the learned material.

If everything celebrates, nothing feels important. If nothing responds, effort feels invisible.

## Product boundaries

- “Done today” and “learned” are different claims.
- Course percentage is structural completion until mastery exists.
- A streak is not mastery, fluency, commitment, or moral worth.
- Review should emerge from attempt evidence and time, not from arbitrary content recycling.
- A notification is an interruption that must earn its cost with relevance, timing, frequency control, and an obvious way to reduce or stop it.
- Marketing may promise the next credible transformation, never a capability the product cannot measure or teach yet.
