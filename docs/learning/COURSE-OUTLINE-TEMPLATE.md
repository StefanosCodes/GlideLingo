## 4. Course-stage outline — required

The stage structure below is a useful beginner-course starting point. A course may rename, combine, split, or replace stages when learner needs and language-specific evidence justify it.

| Stage | Learner transformation | Typical mission families | Required proof |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Access | I can enter the language safely and understand how to participate. | Script or sound orientation, interface language, repair, clarification | Recognize essential contrasts and request help |
| 1. First contact | I can begin and close a simple social interaction. | Greeting, names, introductions, courtesy | Complete a short first meeting |
| 2. Personal world | I can exchange essential information about myself and familiar people or things. | Origin, languages, home, possession, family | Understand and produce a short personal exchange |
| 3. Everyday needs | I can obtain common goods, services, and information. | Café, shopping, prices, availability, requests | Complete one service interaction with repair |
| 4. Place and movement | I can locate places and coordinate simple movement. | Directions, transport, time, lodging | Follow information and reach a stated outcome |
| 5. Routine and plans | I can describe familiar activities and coordinate a near-future plan. | Daily routine, preferences, invitations, scheduling | Create and respond to a simple plan |
| 6. Integrated milestone | I can combine prior capabilities in unfamiliar but bounded situations. | Multi-step scenario, message plus interaction, short narrative | Pass unseen and delayed transfer tasks |

Each course stage must define its own observable exit rather than inheriting the table mechanically.

## 10. Motivation plan — required

For each mechanic, record:

- learning outcome;
- target behavior;
- journey moment;
- intended emotional transition;
- mechanism;
- learner truth made visible;
- learning metric;
- behavior metric;
- harm guardrails;
- opt-out and recovery behavior.

The initial course should use missions, capability milestones, progress proximity, and learner-chosen consistency. Competitive mechanics remain outside the default experience.
