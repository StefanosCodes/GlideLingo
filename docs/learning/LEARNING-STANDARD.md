# GlideLingo Learning Standard

## Product outcome

GlideLingo helps a self-directed learner build durable, usable language capability and understand what to do next.

The system must optimize for language use, retention, learner agency, and calm momentum. Engagement is valuable only when it produces or protects meaningful practice.

## Standard in one sentence

Every course is an authored capability graph delivered through real-world missions, balanced practice, fading support, delayed retrieval, and evidence-backed progress.

## Evidence base and limits

This standard combines complementary bodies of evidence rather than claiming one universal method:

- Paul Nation's [Four Strands](https://www.wgtn.ac.nz/lals/resources/paul-nations-resources/paul-nations-publications/publications/documents/1996-Four-strands.pdf) supports balancing meaning-focused input, language-focused learning, meaning-focused output, and fluency development. The approximate balance is a course-design diagnostic, not a rigid timer for every lesson.
- The Council of Europe's [CEFR](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/) frames learners as social agents and language use as reception, production, interaction, and mediation. CEFR descriptors guide outcomes and alignment; they do not provide a complete language-specific curriculum.
- A meta-analysis of [task-based language teaching](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1362168817744389) supports authentic tasks and interaction as a productive instructional direction.
- A broad review of [spacing and retrieval practice](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-022-00089-1) supports effortful recall distributed over time for durable learning.
- A meta-analysis of [corrective feedback in second-language acquisition](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9922.2010.00561.x) supports timely feedback whose usefulness varies by context and form.
- A meta-analysis of [gamified learning](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w) found small positive average effects with important design and context differences. No mechanic is justified by the statement that gamification works.
- Self-determination theory offers autonomy, competence, and relatedness as a useful motivation lens. It does not predict that any interface labeled with those terms will work. See [Ryan and Deci](https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_SDT.pdf).

Second-language evidence often comes from particular learner populations, target languages, classroom settings, or short interventions. Every consequential claim must be validated in GlideLingo using delayed learning measures, not only completion or retention metrics.

## Non-negotiable invariants

1. **Real-world use is the curriculum atom.** Capabilities describe what a learner can accomplish, not what content they have viewed.
2. **Practice and assessment are aligned but distinct.** A learner must not pass solely by memorizing practice items.
3. **Recognition is scaffolding, not independent production evidence.** Multiple choice and word banks can teach and diagnose; they cannot by themselves establish speaking or writing capability.
4. **Progress claims follow evidence.** Completion, practice, demonstration, and retention are separate states.
5. **Learning survives absence.** A missed day cannot erase completed work, evidence, capability, or learner identity.
6. **Mistakes remain available for learning.** Errors may change feedback and future practice; they never consume access to the lesson.
7. **Personalization is bounded.** It may select or adapt practice within published contracts; it may not redefine the curriculum or mastery rules.
8. **The curriculum spine is authored, reviewed, validated, and versioned.** AI may create candidates but does not publish its own work.
9. **Language-specific truth overrides universal convenience.** Script, sound, grammar, pragmatics, register, variation, and cultural context require explicit language profiles and qualified review.
10. **The product remains humane.** No shame, fake urgency, hostage mechanics, hidden progress rules, compulsory public comparison, or monetized relief from deliberately created learning pressure.

## Universal learning model

### The two graphs

```text
COURSE GRAPH
Capability -> prerequisite -> mission -> lesson -> activity

LEARNER GRAPH
Attempt -> evidence -> capability state -> review need -> retained performance
```

The course graph is published content. The learner graph is durable personal evidence. They can share identifiers but must never become one mutable object.

The learner-facing path is a clear, mostly linear projection over the course graph. Branching appears only when it gives the learner a consequential choice, such as a goal, context, challenge, or recovery route.

### Capability contract

Every capability must define:

- a stable identifier;
- a learner-facing can-do statement;
- a real-world domain and situation;
- applicable communication modes;
- prerequisite capability identifiers;
- required linguistic, pragmatic, and cultural resources;
- minimum practice and evidence opportunities;
- assessment criteria and acceptable support;
- relevant external standard references;
- reviewer and publication provenance.

A capability should be narrow enough to assess and broad enough to matter outside one exercise.

### Mission contract

Every mission must define:

- a concrete communicative job;
- the learner role and other participant roles;
- entry conditions and prerequisites;
- target and supporting capabilities;
- scenario, stakes, and completion condition;
- language resources introduced or recycled;
- planned input, output, interaction, and fluency opportunities;
- checkpoint design using unseen or meaningfully varied material;
- delayed-review targets;
- accessibility and non-speaking alternatives where needed;
- anticipated duration and interruption recovery.

### The learning cycle

Each mission uses the following cycle. A short lesson may implement only part of it, but the complete mission may not omit a stage without justification.

```text
Encounter -> Notice -> Retrieve -> Produce -> Perform -> Revisit
```

1. **Encounter:** Understand useful language in a comprehensible situation.
2. **Notice:** Attend to a relevant sound, spelling, word, grammar, discourse, or pragmatic contrast.
3. **Retrieve:** Recall language without copying it from the prompt.
4. **Produce:** Speak, write, select, manipulate, or construct meaning with progressively less support.
5. **Perform:** Complete the mission using an integrated set of capabilities.
6. **Revisit:** Retrieve the capability later and in a changed context.

Support should fade deliberately:

```text
Model -> constrained choice -> cued recall -> free response -> varied transfer
```

Activities may move backward when evidence shows the learner needs support. The system must explain the recovery path without framing it as punishment.

## Course balance

Balance is evaluated across a module or stage, not forced into each sitting.

### Learning opportunities

| Strand | Learner work | Typical activities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meaning-focused input | Understand messages | Graded dialogue, story, notice, audio, video, reading |
| Language-focused learning | Notice and deliberately study form | Sound contrast, spelling, vocabulary retrieval, grammar pattern, pragmatic explanation |
| Meaning-focused output | Convey a message | Speaking, writing, interaction, information gap, response construction |
| Fluency development | Use familiar language more comfortably | Repeated task, shadowing, timed reread, familiar dialogue variation, faster retrieval |

### Communication coverage

Every stage must declare its coverage of:

- listening;
- reading;
- spoken production;
- written production;
- spoken or written interaction;
- mediation where appropriate;
- repair strategies;
- phonological and orthographic competence;
- sociolinguistic and pragmatic appropriateness.

Not every mission needs every mode. No course may claim balanced general proficiency while systematically omitting a mode.

## Evidence and progress

### Capability states

```text
Unseen -> Introduced -> Practiced -> Demonstrated -> Retained
```

- **Unseen:** no qualifying encounter.
- **Introduced:** the learner encountered the capability with instruction or high support.
- **Practiced:** the learner produced qualifying attempts with support.
- **Demonstrated:** the learner met the capability criteria in an unseen or meaningfully varied checkpoint with bounded support.
- **Retained:** the learner demonstrated it again after a meaningful delay or in a transfer context.

These states are not a universal scoring formula. Each capability defines the evidence it needs, and deterministic policy interprets attempts.

### Evidence dimensions

An attempt is interpreted using at least:

- capability and published activity version;
- communication mode;
- response and result;
- support and hints used;
- prompt and context familiarity;
- latency or fluency when valid and accessible;
- delay since prior exposure;
- feedback and retry sequence;
- device or provider limitations affecting interpretation.

For speech, recognition confidence and pronunciation quality are separate constructs. GlideLingo must not present phoneme-level or accent-quality claims until the evaluator has been validated for that target language and learner population.

### Progress presentation

The product keeps these views distinct:

- **Path progress:** lessons or missions completed.
- **Capability progress:** capabilities introduced, demonstrated, and retained.
- **Skill profile:** evidence by mode and communicative dimension.
- **Consistency:** meaningful practice against the learner's chosen cadence.
- **Review state:** what needs retrieval and why it returned.

A single percentage may describe structural path completion. It must not be labeled fluency, mastery, or proficiency.

## Assessment standard

Assessment primarily supports learning. The [CEFR assessment guidance](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/assessment) likewise places emphasis on what learners can demonstrate and on using assessment to inform learning.

Every stage includes:

1. low-cost formative checks with immediate informational feedback;
2. a mission checkpoint with material not memorized from the lesson;
3. delayed retrieval or transfer checks;
4. separate reporting by applicable communication mode;
5. clear criteria for support, retry, and evidence interpretation.

Practice item authors must not approve their own assessment alignment. Assessment items need independent review and must be versioned separately enough to prevent accidental practice leakage.

## Personalization standard

Personalization chooses the next useful action by considering:

- published prerequisites;
- learner goal and chosen context;
- time since successful retrieval;
- recent errors and recovery;
- support required;
- neglected communication modes;
- new-content versus consolidation balance;
- learner time and device constraints.

The first implementation should be deterministic and explainable. Model-generated practice may be added only after typed boundaries, safety rules, traces, and stable evaluation cases exist.

Personalization optimizes learning opportunity. Engagement probability may break ties but may not override prerequisite, review, accessibility, or evidence rules.

## Motivation and gameful design

### North star: calm momentum

The learner should feel oriented, capable, curious, and welcomed back. Emotional intensity scales with the meaning and rarity of the event.

### Approved initial mechanics

- real-world missions with a visible finish;
- clear progress and proximity to the next capability;
- specific informational feedback;
- capability milestones naming what changed;
- learner-chosen weekly consistency goals;
- transparent flexibility and warm lapse recovery;
- personal bests tied to less support, better transfer, or greater fluency;
- a private portfolio of produced language artifacts;
- optional cooperative practice and encouragement;
- content-based humor, narrative, and surprise.

### Deferred mechanics

These require a separate hypothesis, opt-out behavior, learning metric, harm guardrails, and experiment before implementation:

- public leaderboards or leagues;
- competitive ranking;
- generalized points or XP;
- badges unrelated to capability;
- virtual currencies and shops;
- daily quests;
- paid or random streak repair;
- reward multipliers;
- high-frequency notifications.

### Prohibited mechanics

- restricting learning access because the learner made mistakes;
- erasing mastery or completed work after absence;
- shame, threats, fake urgency, or relationship simulation;
- fabricated people, activity, scarcity, or social proof;
- compulsory public exposure of learning performance;
- confusing or random paid outcomes;
- progress inflation designed to produce activity rather than truth.

### Consistency mechanic brief

- **Learning outcome:** more distributed meaningful practice and delayed retrieval.
- **Target behavior:** complete the next useful learning action on a learner-chosen number of days per week.
- **Journey moment:** planning, before practice, and return after a pause.
- **Emotional transition:** from uncertainty or guilt to continuity and agency.
- **Mechanism:** visible weekly rhythm, planned flexibility, preserved capability, and a small re-entry task.
- **Candidate copy:** "You planned three practice days this week. Two are complete." After a lapse: "Your learning is still here. Continue with a short review."
- **Guardrails:** no midnight reset theater, no lost learning, no purchase required for recovery, no claim that the count proves ability.
- **Primary learning metric:** delayed capability performance.
- **Primary behavior metric:** meaningful practice days against chosen cadence.
- **Harm metrics:** post-lapse abandonment, rushed minimal sessions, notification disablement, and learner-reported pressure.

## Language-specific course profile

Universal structure must not flatten language differences. Every course defines a reviewed profile covering:

- target language, locale, variety, and register policy;
- learner's instruction language and assumed prior knowledge;
- writing system and transliteration policy;
- sound system and pronunciation priorities;
- morphology and syntax relevant to the target level;
- lexical frequency and usefulness sources;
- formulaic sequences and discourse conventions;
- politeness, address, gesture, and pragmatic norms;
- regional variation and accepted alternatives;
- cultural contexts, representation, and sensitive topics;
- speech, text-to-speech, keyboard, and accessibility constraints;
- language-specific certification or proficiency references;
- required native-speaker and specialist reviewers.

The same mission may appear across languages, but its prerequisites, language resources, interaction pattern, and appropriate response cannot be translated mechanically.

## Content authoring and publication

### Human and AI roles

AI may:

- draft capability candidates;
- propose examples, contrasts, feedback, and practice variants;
- create dialogue and scenario candidates;
- prepare audio scripts;
- run deterministic validation and coverage reports.

AI may not:

- publish its own content;
- silently change the course graph;
- certify language naturalness or cultural appropriateness;
- certify assessment validity;
- invent progress evidence;
- make official pronunciation claims without validated language-specific evaluation.

### Review gates

Published content requires:

1. schema and reference validation;
2. capability and prerequisite review;
3. instructional-design review;
4. target-language linguistic and pragmatic review;
5. independent assessment review;
6. audio and platform QA;
7. accessibility review;
8. pilot evidence appropriate to the claim;
9. immutable publication version and provenance.

The authoring workflow begins with file-backed source and deterministic validation. A content-management studio is earned only after the editorial workflow is understood.

## Measurement standard

### Learning north star

**Retained capabilities gained per learner-hour**, based on delayed and meaningfully varied performance.

This is a design direction, not a metric to publish before reliable capability criteria, assessment coverage, and sufficient observation windows exist.

### Learning measures

- unseen mission performance;
- delayed retrieval success;
- correction on retry;
- transfer to a changed context;
- production with decreasing support;
- balanced growth across applicable modes;
- assessor agreement for human-rated work.

### Behavior measures

- meaningful practice days;
- mission start-to-finish rate;
- voluntary next useful action;
- return after a pause;
- due-review completion;
- help and recovery use.

### Guardrail measures

- point or activity farming;
- recognition-only avoidance of production;
- repeated speaking opt-out;
- frustration and repeated error loops;
- post-lapse abandonment;
- notification disablement;
- accessibility failures;
- performance gaps across target languages, platforms, accents, and learner groups;
- progress claims not reproducible from stored evidence.

## Definition of done for a course stage

A course stage is publishable only when:

- every mission names observable outcomes and prerequisites;
- every target capability has teaching, practice, immediate assessment, and delayed evidence opportunities;
- the coverage report shows an intentional balance across the four strands and applicable communication modes;
- practice and checkpoint item pools are separated;
- language and pragmatic review is complete;
- audio and text assets resolve and pass QA;
- every activity has accessible instructions and recovery behavior;
- deterministic validation passes;
- pilot results support the learner-facing claims;
- the published version is immutable and traceable.
