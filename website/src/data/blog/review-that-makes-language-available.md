Review often becomes a place where learning piles up. Every word, rule, and exercise returns until the queue feels more like maintenance than progress.

Useful review has a narrower purpose. It helps a skill become available at the moment you need it.

## Recall before recognition

Recognition can feel fluent because the answer is already in front of you. Recall asks more of memory. You have to produce the word, meaning, or response from a cue.

Both forms have value, but recall is closer to conversation. A speaker does not choose from four visible answers. They hear a cue and build the next turn.

Begin review with a genuine attempt. Then reveal support if you need it. The effort to retrieve is part of what strengthens the path back to the language.

## Review the weak connection

A mistake does not always mean the whole idea is missing. You may understand a phrase when you read it but not when you hear it. You may know the meaning but hesitate over the form.

Good review identifies the connection that failed. It can then present the same skill from the angle that needs work instead of restarting the entire lesson.

This keeps correction proportional. It also makes progress easier to notice because the next attempt tests the part that actually changed.

## Let successful material wait

Items that return too quickly can create the illusion of mastery. The answer is still active, so the task feels easy.

Spacing gives memory a chance to cool. When the skill returns later, successful recall provides stronger evidence that it is becoming durable.

The interval does not have to be perfect. The useful principle is simple: bring fragile material back sooner and let demonstrated material wait longer.

## Return skills to context

Review should not trap language in isolated cards. Once a word or structure becomes familiar, put it back into a sentence, a listening cue, or a small exchange.

Context changes the task from “Do I know this?” to “Can I use this here?” That second question is closer to the outcome learners care about.

Variation matters too. Keep the skill stable while changing the speaker, setting, or intention. This helps the learner recognize the pattern without depending on one memorized example.

## End with evidence

A review session should answer a concrete question. What became easier to recall? What still needs support? What can now wait?

Visible evidence prevents review from feeling endless. It turns a queue of old material into a record of strengthening skills.

The purpose of review is not to keep every lesson permanently present. It is to make useful language easier to reach, one connection at a time.
